## 4. Inaccessible solvers and reasons

- **Not installed:** FICO Xpress and Hexaly (commercial, no licence available); Choco (needs a Java runtime, which the host lacks). They were not evaluated, and no substitute was used.
- **Licence-capped:** CP Optimizer, Gurobi and CPLEX were run only within their free-edition limits. They refuse 15×15 and 20×20 models: the 30 attempted paper-scale cold runs are recorded as licence refusals, not as performance.
- No licence restriction was bypassed.

## 5. Mathematical model equivalence

See `docs/multisolver/MODEL_EQUIVALENCE.md`.

- **Shared inputs:** one canonical instance and identical domains [ℓ_i, u_i].
- **Three formulations:**
  - interval models (CP-SAT, CP Optimizer);
  - MiniZinc `disjunctive` (Gecode, Chuffed);
  - one disjunctive MILP translated verbatim to six APIs.
- **Big-M:** M_ij = u_i + p_i − ℓ_j, the smallest constant valid for all bounded schedules. Order-implied pairs get no binary; outage binaries are created only when both sides are possible.
- **Restriction rows:** C ≤ U−1 and kept arcs s_v ≥ s_u + p_u are added identically to every formulation.
- **Model size at 20×20:** MILP 4,607 variables / 8,800 constraints vs CP-SAT 831 variables (5.5×).
- **Scope limit:** positive durations only (campaign durations ≥ 5). Zero-length operations inside outages are treated differently by CP-SAT; this is documented.

## 6. Exact-oracle verification

`tests/multisolver/test_equivalence.py` (183 tests) checks every engine against `exhaustive_optimum` on 16 instances. The oracle enumerates machine orders and uses no solver code. The instances cover:
- plain shops;
- releases;
- outages;
- commitments;
- arrival, cancellation, duration and outage deltas.

The tests also check that every engine returns INFEASIBLE exactly when the cutoff is below the optimum, that kept arcs are enforced, and that no MILP row cuts an oracle-optimal schedule. The smoke run (`run_smoke.py`) did 624 method-level runs with 0 failures.

Post-hoc validation (`results/multisolver/validation_report.json`): 16,944 of 16,944 held-out OK records were re-validated (schedule, makespan = objective, trajectory ordering, events within the budget plus overrun), with 0 violations. The 674 development-pilot records written before final schedules were stored are listed separately and are not used in any held-out statistic.

Test suite: 462 fast (including the 183 equivalence tests and the strict-improvement test) + 25 slow = 487 passed.

**Experiment C (development-selected configurations):**

## 8. Warm-start versus external-incumbent results

**RQ-S2 and RQ-S3.**
- **S2 vs S0** (previous schedule retained externally): lowers PI for all 10 engines on 8×6, significant for each (−39.5% to −83.6%). At paper scale it is lower in every stream for all 7 engines.
- **S3 vs S2** (full portfolio): significant for 9 of 10 engines on 8×6 (CP-SAT n.s., CI excludes zero), and lower in every stream at paper scale.
- **S1 vs S0** (native warm start):
  - significant gains for Gurobi (−29.6%), CPLEX (−56.6%), SCIP (−54.0%) and HiGHS (−53.9%);
  - no effect for CBC's OR-Tools hint (+0.1%);
  - higher PI for CP-SAT (+29.6%, CI [+5.9, +62.5]) and CP Optimizer (+8.4%, CI [+1.0, +15.1]); n.s. under Holm. The CP-SAT effect is significant in the contention-filtered sensitivity analysis.
- **S2 vs S1** (same schedule, external vs native): significant for CP-SAT, CP Optimizer, Gurobi, CPLEX and CBC; not for SCIP or HiGHS.
- **Conclusion:** the early validated incumbent transfers across technologies; a particular warm-start API does not.

## 10. Computational resource differences

Supplement Table S4 and Figure S6.

- **Model build plus API translation at 20×20:** 0.009 s for CP-SAT vs 0.03–0.16 s across MIP APIs (0.16 s for SCIP).
- **Peak RSS:** up to 321 MB (CBC, 20×20) vs 112 MB (CP-SAT).
- **Deadline overruns:** CBC overran its time limit by up to 6.4 s at 20×20, HiGHS by 2.4 s and GLPK by 4.2 s. Incumbents returned late earn no credit, so this costs those engines PI and does not inflate anything.
- **Four workers:** CP-SAT CPU/wall 3.3, Gecode 3.1.
- **Host contention:** the host was shared with an unrelated CPU-bound workload (14 `xsimk` processes; median 1-minute load 23 on 20 logical CPUs) that this work neither started nor stopped. Median single-worker CPU/wall was 0.97; 9% of runs fell below 0.8.
  - Pre-declared sensitivity analysis (`analysis/heldout_cpu08.json`): 6 of 226 significance decisions change, all through a single stream flipping (n unchanged). No result significant in either analysis reverses sign.

## 11. Statistical evidence

- **Unit of inference:** the stream. Steps and seeds are averaged within a stream.
- **Test:** exact two-sided sign test with Holm correction within each frozen family. 168 comparisons are confirmatory (A, B, C) and 58 exploratory (R2, R30, W4).
- **CI:** 10,000-resample stream bootstrap of the mean paired difference, scaled by the comparator mean. This is the CP-SAT study's convention, so intervals can extend below −100%.
- **Significance:** Holm p < 0.05 and a CI excluding zero.
- **Power limitation:** with 5 streams the smallest attainable two-sided p is 0.0625, so no 15×15 or 20×20 comparison can be significant under the frozen rule. Those results are reported descriptively: direction in every stream, plus CI.
  - This is a limitation of the frozen design. The plan was not changed after the data were seen.
- **Post-hoc analyses:** `attribute_stages.py` and `posthoc_comparisons.py` were chosen after the held-out results were seen and are labelled as such everywhere.

## 12. Negative results

**CP-SAT study**
- Unstructured repetition alone adds almost nothing (−2.5%, n.s.).
- Critical-region targeting is not established as superior to time-window targeting.
- The four-worker advantage is not established.
- The certificate study gave sound bounds but no decision value.

**Cross-solver study**
- Restricted rounds significantly *hurt* CP Optimizer on 8×6, and the effect recurs at 2 s and 30 s.
- Restricted rounds are not significant for CP-SAT, Gecode and the commercial MIP engines on 8×6.
- They do not help CP-SAT at four workers on 8×6.
- They barely help CBC and GLPK at 20×20.
- A CP-SAT solution hint does not improve on a cold start; its point estimate is worse.
- CBC's OR-Tools hint has no effect.
- Paper-scale comparisons are underpowered by design (5 streams).

## 13. Generalization limitations

- **Instances:** synthetic full job shops from one generator, with one delta per step; disruption severity was not varied in the cross-solver study.
- **Commercial engines:** evaluated only on 8×6 because of free-edition caps. Conclusions about them at paper scale cannot be drawn.
- **Formulations:** one per technology, so engine differences are differences between engine–model pairs.
- **Initialization:** not uniform. Gecode, Chuffed and GLPK accept no warm start; restricted rounds are hinted only for CP-SAT and CP Optimizer.
- **Trajectories:** CBC and GLPK trajectories are observable only on return.
- **z\*:** the best value found, not a proven optimum.
- **Host:** contended (see section 10).
- **Versions:** results are version-specific (OR-Tools 9.15, etc.).
